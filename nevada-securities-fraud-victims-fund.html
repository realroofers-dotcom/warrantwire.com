Nevada now has a fund for victims of securities fraud

If a Nevada court ordered restitution to you in a securities case brought by the state, and the money was never fully paid, you may be able to ask the state for part of it: up to $25,000, or 25% of what is unpaid, whichever is less.

What it is

The Fund for the Compensation of Victims of Securities Fraud. Nevada created it in Senate Bill 76 of 2025. It is now part of Nevada's securities law, NRS chapter 90, at sections 90.701 to 90.709. It took effect for all purposes on January 1, 2026 (SB 76, section 13).

It pays from a state fund, not from the wrongdoer. The fund is fed by gifts, grants and donations the Division accepts, interest earned, and money paid back by people who later recover (NRS 90.705, 90.710).

Who can apply

"a person to whom a court orders restitution pursuant to NRS 90.640 may, not later than 12 months after the date on which such restitution is ordered, apply to the Administrator for compensation from the Fund."NRS 90.703(1)

In plain English: you need a restitution order from a Nevada court under NRS 90.640. That is the section that lets a Nevada district court, when the state's Securities Administrator shows that someone broke Nevada's securities law, order remedies including "restitution to investors" (NRS 90.640(1)(a)(4)).

The Securities Division sets the rest of the eligibility rules by regulation (NRS 90.708(1)). The statute itself does not say an applicant must live in Nevada. Check the Division's regulations before relying on anything.

Older or vulnerable investors: under NRS 90.640, if the violation was committed against an older person or a vulnerable person, the restitution ordered must be twice what it would otherwise be.

How much

"such compensation must not exceed the lesser of $25,000 or an amount equal to 25 percent of the amount of unpaid restitution awarded by a court."NRS 90.708(4)

Example (arithmetic only): unpaid restitution of $60,000 → 25% is $15,000, so the most is $15,000. Unpaid restitution of $200,000 → 25% is $50,000, so the cap of $25,000 applies.

The deadline

Apply within 12 months after the date the court orders restitution. The Administrator may waive the time limit "for good cause shown" (NRS 90.703(1)). If you are turned down, you can appeal under the Division's regulations (NRS 90.703(3)).

Your application stays private

What you submit is confidential. It may be disclosed only at your (or your lawyer's) request, in the necessary administration of the law, or on a court order (NRS 90.703(5)).

If you later collect any of the unpaid restitution, you must tell the Division and pay the fund back the smaller of what the fund paid you or what you recovered, less lawyer's fees and costs. The State steps into your right to collect, up to the amount it paid (NRS 90.707).

How a case starts

  1. A complaint to the Nevada Securities Division (Office of the Secretary of State, nvsos.gov: Securities → File a Complaint). Anyone who believes they were defrauded in a Nevada securities matter can file.
  2. The Division investigates. It has subpoena power (NRS 90.620). Complaints and investigation files are confidential, and the Division generally will not tell you whether it is investigating (NRS 90.730).
  3. The Administrator can go to court. A Nevada district court can order injunctions, civil penalties of up to $25,000 per violation ($50,000 against an older or vulnerable person), and restitution to investors (NRS 90.640). Criminal cases can be referred for prosecution (NRS 90.650).
  4. Only then the fund. If restitution is ordered and not paid, the investor can apply to the fund within 12 months.

What it is not

  • It is not automatic. No court restitution order, no fund.
  • It does not pay ordinary market losses, and it does not replace a lawsuit. The Division cannot promise anyone's money back and does not give legal advice.
  • Whether a matter falls under Nevada's securities law at all depends on where the offer or sale happened (NRS 90.830).
