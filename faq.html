Questions answered

What is free, what costs money, and why.

Short version: everything we can send you for nothing is free. The one thing that costs us money every single time we send it — a text message — is the one thing you pay for.

Why is the text the one you pay for?

Because a text goes through the phone companies, and they charge us for every single message, every time. Nothing else here does. We would rather charge the people who want a text than quietly take it out of everybody else, and we would rather tell you that plainly than dress it up.

The work behind the warning is the same whichever way it reaches you. Somebody read the filing. What you are paying for is the speed, not the reading.

What does the text cost?

Nothing extra. It comes with a yearly subscription — take a year and the texts are part of it. We do not sell them on their own.

Texts take two days to switch on. That is not us being slow: the phone companies have to register the sender before anybody may send a text message to the public, and they take about two days over it.

You will be told the day your texts are live.

I have an iPhone. Can I get the free phone notice?

Sorry — no. We send the phone notice on Android as a courtesy, and we would do the same on an iPhone if we were allowed to. Apple does not permit a website to send a notification to an iPhone at all unless you first add that site to your Home Screen. Their regulation, not ours, and we cannot argue with it.

So on an iPhone there are three honest ways to be told:

  • The email — free, today, nothing to install.
  • A yearly subscription, which includes the text. Nothing to install for that either, and it reaches you first.
  • Add Warrant Wire to your Home Screen — tap Share, then Add to Home Screen, then open it from that icon. The free notice then works exactly as it does on Android. But we are not going to make you install anything before we will warn you.

And if you are choosing a phone: on Android this is free and takes one tap.

What do you do with my email or my number?

We send you what you asked for and nothing else. We do not sell it, rent it, or hand it to anybody. There is no advertising network on this site and no tracking pixels. Every message has one line in it to stop the whole thing.

The page of last week's searches

Why is it a week old?

On purpose. A week-old count of who was asking about a company is history — you cannot trade on it, and we are not going to pretend you can. Published the same day it would be a live signal with our name on it, put there by strangers, and that is what a pump board is. We are the opposite of that, or we are nothing.

Then what is it for?

It shows you honestly what this wire is being asked about and how often the wire finds something. It is also the plainest argument we have for paying: everything on that page reached the people who pay the day it happened.

Can anyone see what I searched for?

No. We publish which companies are being asked about. We never publish who asked, and nothing on this site can tell anybody that a particular person looked at a particular company. That is set out in full on the privacy page, including the wording we changed on 8 November 2026 and why — we left the old wording on the page rather than quietly replacing it.

The reports

What do I get for nothing?

The five most recent filings on the wire every day; for any company, how many filings it has made, how many are heavy, which terms are in them and who signed; the grades; how a verdict is rendered; and the warnings in plain words — a name change, a reverse split, a merger on the table. The full list is on the price page.

What costs money?

The detail. One company for a year — every filing, the verdict, the rules that fired and its going concern status. The going concern list. The founder's verdict, written by Mark Nejmeh himself. And at 8K10Q, our sister site, any single filing read in full in plain English, every section and not only the warrants.

What is the difference between Warrant Wire and 8K10Q?

Warrant Wire reads the warrant paper. It watches every filing for the language of dilution, resets, inducements, reverse splits and mergers, and tells you when it finds it. 8K10Q reads the whole filing — every section, in plain English, not only the warrants — and it has the rooms, where people talk about what they are seeing under their real names. Same data, same people, two jobs.

One thing we will not do. We do not sell anything we have not built. If something on this site is not running yet, it says so on the page rather than taking your money and hoping. Opinions, never advice: nothing here tells you to buy or sell anything, and a company being asked about says nothing at all about that company.
