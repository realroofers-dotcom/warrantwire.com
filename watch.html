Reverse-merger watch

A reverse merger can end a company as its stockholders know it: a private company takes over the listing, a flood of new shares is issued, and the board and management are replaced. It rarely comes out of nowhere. The steps before it — a strategic review, a reverse split, new authorized shares, a rights plan, a letter of intent — are filed with the SEC first. Every morning we read the filings of the companies you watch, and email you the day one of those steps appears.

$100 a year, per company. Name the company; we watch every filing it makes for a year and email you when one of the signs below appears, with the filing, the words we found in it, and a line on why it matters.

A signal is a reason to read, not an accusation. Everything on this list is lawful and common. A reverse split may be needed to keep a listing; a material agreement may be good news. The watch tells you it happened, the day it happened. You decide what it means.

Public filings only. Every alert comes from a filing on the SEC’s EDGAR system and links to it. Nothing here is investment advice.

What we watch for

Three levels. Red means the thing itself has been filed. Amber means a step that often comes before it. Watch means worth reading, routine on its own.

Red

  • Merger filings — S-4, merger proxy, Rule 425 communications
  • Rights plan (poison pill) — 8-K Item 3.03, a new class on Form 8-A
  • Change in control — 8-K Item 5.01
  • Assets bought or sold — 8-K Item 2.01
  • Tender offers — SC TO, SC 14D-9
  • Delisting or deregistration — Form 25, Form 15
  • Successor company — Form 8-K12, as after a reverse merger

Amber

  • Material agreement signed — 8-K Item 1.01, read for which kind
  • Charter amended — 8-K Item 5.03: reverse splits, new authorized shares
  • Listing warning — 8-K Item 3.01
  • Shares or warrants sold privately — 8-K Item 3.02
  • Preliminary proxy — PRE 14A: splits, share increases, warrant votes
  • Offerings — 424B prospectuses, often units with warrants
  • 5% holder with intent — Schedule 13D

Watch

  • Directors or officers changed — 8-K Item 5.02
  • Shareholder vote results — 8-K Item 5.07
  • Other events — 8-K Item 8.01, where strategic reviews are announced
  • New debt — 8-K Item 2.03
  • Proxy statements and registrations — DEF 14A, S-1, S-3

We also read the text of the filings that matter for words such as reverse merger, merger agreement, rights agreement, reverse stock split, letter of intent, strategic alternatives, pre-funded warrant and foreclosure. A word found in the text can raise an alert’s level; it never lowers it.
