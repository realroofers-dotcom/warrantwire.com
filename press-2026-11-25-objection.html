Nejmeh Files Emergency OBJECTION in Biotech Case

Theriva (TOVX) got more time in under 24 hours. Its reverse split takes effect December 1.

November 25, 2026 – Mark Nejmeh, a Theriva Biologics (NYSE American: TOVX) stockholder suing the company and four of its directors, today filed an emergency objection asking the District Judge to set aside an order that gave Theriva more time to answer his complaint, with no fixed end date (Nejmeh v. Theriva Biologics, Inc., No. 3:26-cv-00705, D. Nev., ECF No. 63).

Theriva’s request was entered the evening of November 23 and granted at midday November 24, less than 24 hours later and before Mr. Nejmeh’s time to respond had run. That morning, Theriva’s lawyers had offered in writing to answer by December 1 if he gave up any further amendment; he declined. Hours later, trading in TOVX was halted for trading below ten cents. Theriva’s motion mentioned neither.

Theriva’s 1-for-30 reverse split takes effect at 11:59 p.m. Eastern on December 1. The Court set a status conference for December 7. The objection asks that it be held before December 1.

In October, while his request for an injunction was pending, Theriva sold SYN-004, a drug that met its Phase 2b goal and held FDA Breakthrough Therapy designation, for $350,000 upfront to Biossil, a Toronto startup the Globe and Mail described as aiming to “give failed drugs new life with AI.” SYN-004 had not failed. Mr. Nejmeh believes promising drugs are being taken cheap and relabeled as AI discoveries, delaying treatment while patients wait.

“This is the most important filing, because it shows we fight and do not quit,” said Mr. Nejmeh, founder of 8K10Q.com.

Theriva reports a $365.4 million accumulated deficit, financed “principally” by issuing stock. The claims are allegations; no court has ruled on them. The filings and a plain-English summary are at nejmehvstheriva.com.
