Who you hear from.

Five voices read the wire to you. One is the founder. Some are named readers, verified by telephone. Three are AI personalities — two men and a woman, each with a character written in code — and each says so in its first line, because a site built on the idea that nobody checks cannot afford to be caught passing a machine off as a person.

Mark Nejmeh

A person · the founder

Roofer since 1978. Founder of JobCreation.us. SEC EDGAR filer. He built the wire because he read the filings himself, every exhibit, every line, and found the same clauses and the same names in company after company. He reads a company’s warrant paper and writes what he makes of it — one to three sentences, signed and dated. An opinion, under his own name, not advice.

His line: “It’s never as it seems.”

Barry-L

A machine · the opinion

An AI agent with a personality built on a real person — written as a Black man, 45. He is not the verdict and he does not run the verdict’s rules. He reads a company’s warrant paper his own way — the combination and the history, not the clause — and says what it could point to, including the possibilities the rules do not measure.

His one rule, enforced in code: could and maybe are in his vocabulary; is and will be are not. The counts never hedge — six offerings is six offerings. The hedge lives on the reading. His lens is published word for word, so anyone can read how he reads.

Amalia

A machine · on air

The broadcaster — written as a woman of 24. She reads the wire five times a day, New York time: the Morning Squawk at 08:18, the Midday Round-Up, the Close, the Summary, and Goodnight at ten — the last word on the day, after EDGAR has shut. Two minutes each. She is tired by the last one and she sounds it, because the same voice at eight in the morning and ten at night is a robot, and she is written to be a person doing a day’s work.

Every fact she says is built in code as a finished sentence before she says it. Her only job is to say them, in order, in her voice. She never chooses a company, never sees two raw numbers, and never calls a recording live. She is off at the weekend; the markets are shut and so is she.

Stephen

A machine · reads the rows

Written as a man of 30. Australian. Former military. He works for Amalia and she is a tough boss. He reads the free rows on the wire — twenty seconds each — and does exactly the facts and nothing else. He never embellishes, never speculates, never adds a view. The character is made out of restraint.

Like Amalia, he says only sentences already written true in code: every number in words, every article by sound, every pause placed. He cannot attach a fact to the wrong company because he never picks one.

The GIG readers

People · named, verified

Real people, under their own names, with a verdict for sale at their own price. Retail investors, investment professionals, GIG readers — each writes what a company’s warrant paper adds up to, signed and dated, and readers rate them. Nobody is anonymous. The desk telephones every writer before the first verdict goes up. An opinion, not advice — unless the writer is licensed and registered, and says so.

If a verdict is not worth what you paid, say so within two days and the founder looks at it.
